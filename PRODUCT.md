# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

- Viewers who browse tipster profiles and public predictions.
- Tipsters who publish predictions and manage their public profiles.
- Streamers who want to show selected public predictions in streaming software.
- The platform creator who manually approves tipsters.

## Product Purpose

Fijas en vivo helps viewers discover tipsters, review their public predictions and results, and follow creators they trust. Tipsters can choose which of their predictions to feature on a live-stream overlay.

## Positioning

An open tipster directory built around manually approved publishers and public, attributable predictions.

## Operating Context

Tipsters manage profiles and predictions in a browser dashboard. A streamer can add a public browser-source URL to OBS or compatible streaming software and display selected predictions during a broadcast.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The current application uses static HTML, Firebase Authentication, and Cloud Firestore.
- Tipster access is manually approved. Verified accounts own their profiles and predictions.
- Profiles and predictions are public in the current MVP.
- Viewers can register and sign in with email/password or Google, keep a local persistent session, and follow approved tipsters. Password recovery is available in viewer, tipster and owner access forms. Email registration requires legal consent and email verification. The Following feed combines real-time prediction queries in groups of at most 10 tipsters.
- Private viewer documents live in `users`; `follows` stores unique follower/tipster relationships. Each relationship change atomically updates the tipster's follower count, enforced by Firestore rules.
- New accounts must accept the current terms and privacy notice and self-confirm the applicable minimum age; acceptance is versioned and enforced for account actions through Firestore rules.
- Legal documents are an initial Peru-focused draft for review, not a guarantee of liability protection or global legal compliance. Complete operator/contact details and obtain legal review before public launch.
- The project is intended to remain on Firebase Spark for current testing. It does not use Firebase Storage or Cloud Functions.
- The streaming overlay must work as a transparent browser source and must not require a native plugin.
- Predictions selected for the overlay remain public, as do other published predictions.
- The current test stage offers every available overlay format for free.
- Paid access and subscription enforcement are not active.
- Tipster history indicates editability with a green open-lock or amber closed-lock icon, not result status alone. A hover, keyboard-focus or touch tooltip explains the reason; OBS remains independent of this lock. Existing editing restrictions are unchanged.
- The tipster workspace includes history-only search and quick filters (all, pending, started pending results, settled and OBS). They combine with the selected bankroll period/filters without changing financial totals or exports. “Por resolver” means pending with an event start in the past, not proof the event has finished. Publication and eligible edits require a native review dialog with public terms and separately labelled private money; cancelling retains the draft, confirming revalidates the future date before the existing transaction. The tipster Monetization section is an inactive preparation surface, with no checkout, activation handler, paid entitlement, price promise or financial writes. The administrator's existing future revenue-share preparation remains separate; real payments still require backend authorization, verified webhooks, accounting, a provider and legal readiness.
- The private Bankroll tab records one immutable opening per PEN/USD/EUR currency at `users/{uid}/bankrollAccounts/{currency}` (`currency`, integer `initialMinorUnits` from 0 to 100000000000, server `created_at`), then append-only `movements` (`type`: deposit/withdrawal, positive integer `amountMinorUnits` up to 100000000000, `note` up to 200 characters, server `created_at`). Reads/creates require the verified, active, approved owner, an existing base user with matching UID and current legal consent; legacy `users.role` does not grant permissions. Movements also require a valid existing opening. No admin/public access, update or delete is granted. Review is inline before writes; transaction retries retain the same document ID and check an already committed record. Corrections require an explicit opposite movement, never editing history. These local rules passed the focused security review without high-confidence findings; they are not deployed or a security certification.
- The private movements CSV exports only the selected currency's opening and manual movements, ordered by server timestamp then document ID, with ISO 8601 UTC dates, signed two-decimal amounts, private notes and cumulative manual flow. This flow excludes pick profit and risk and is neither equity nor a real bank balance. Export runs locally, includes no account email, uses UTF-8 BOM/CRLF and escaped/formula-protected text, and refuses invalid or partial records. It remains disabled without an opening, committed complete data, legal access, or while reviewing/saving; logout, unmount and currency changes clear export feedback. Existing period-based pick CSV/PDF exports are unchanged.
- Bankroll accounting is manual, without payments, conversion or backend financial/entitlement claims. Saldo contable (equity) = opening + deposits − withdrawals + private pick profit; disponible contable = equity − pending risk, including negative values. A dedicated lifetime query uses `user_id` and `created_at > opening`, ordered by descending creation time (existing index), not the history's period-limited picks; only strictly later picks count and each currency has its own opening boundary. Missing private amounts are counted as excluded, theoretical winning returns and recorded cashout returns are labelled, and incomplete/cache/pending/error snapshots never display partial balances. The separate module mounts after approved/legal dashboard access and stops/clears on logout, revocation or SPA unmount. Existing period reports/exports and public picks are unchanged.
- The viewer frontpage has a sticky glass navbar, a collapsible featured-tipster sidebar, and a live public feed of at most 60 recent predictions. Sports categories (Todos, Fútbol, Baloncesto, Tenis, E-Sports, Otros) filter predictions in Explore, Following and public profiles, not tipster specializations. Featured channels indicate connection to the platform, not a verified video broadcast. Public feed subscriptions reuse groups of at most 10 approved tipsters; adding sports is centralized in the frontpage SPORTS configuration, while new stored sport identifiers still require server-rule support.
- The viewer shell fills the viewport with independently scrolling channels, feed and a pick-scoped community mural on desktop. Below 901px, channels become a collapsible horizontal avatar rail and the mural opens in a native modal drawer. Each approved pick has its own public thread, limited to the latest 50 comments; verified, active viewers with current terms acceptance may post once every 10 seconds. The pick owner and admins with the explicit `community` permission may delete comments and mute an author in that thread. Firestore Rules block known external links and enforce the interval atomically; the configurable blocked-term list is a best-effort client filter and can be bypassed by modified clients. There is no global profile wall. Legal links remain inside the scrollable feed.
- Private comment reports use `picks/{pickId}/comments/{commentId}/commentReports/{reporterUid}`: path segments, not ambiguous concatenated IDs, guarantee one immutable report per account/comment. Verified non-anonymous active accounts with an existing server-managed user, current terms/privacy acceptance and confirmed minimum age can report another author's existing comment on an approved pick. Categories are `spam`, `harassment`, `personal_data`, `misleading`, `other`; optional private `detail` is bounded to 300 characters. Rules validate the real pick/owner and exact source `authorUid`, `authorName`, `text`, bind `reporterUid` to Auth, reject self reports, extra fields and client timestamps, and require server `created_at`. Legacy `users.role` confers no report/admin authority. Reporting does not hide content, mute accounts, report picks, sanction globally or rank by report count.
- Report evidence is never cascade-deleted with its comment, pick or author. Only the reporter may get their deterministic document/status (not list others); community administrators, including the standard super-admin ACL, may read the private `commentReports` collection group. Pick owners without this ACL cannot see report identities, details or counts. Reporters cannot update/delete. The community panel prioritizes this review queue and preserves the existing blacklist; its status-filtered descending-date query displays up to 50 records/page with cursor pagination and no claimed total. `firestore.indexes.json` includes the collection-group `status ASC, created_at DESC` index.
- Admin review is inline and transitions only `pending -> dismissed | removed | unavailable`. Optional private `resolution_reason` is bounded to 300; rules require server `reviewed_at`, authenticated `reviewed_by` and immutable source/report fields, and forbid terminal reopening/deletion. Removing a present comment is one transaction with the audited `removed` resolution, enforced by `exists`/`existsAfter`; an already absent comment becomes `unavailable`, never a fabricated deletion or recreation. Other reports on that comment retain evidence and remain independently reviewable. Community ACL still permits reading/resolving orphan evidence after pick deletion/revocation. Concurrent resolutions explicitly report a conflict; Auth/thread generations, busy submits and unmount/revocation cleanup protect private drafts and queue data. `dismissed` requires the source comment to exist before and after the write, so a missing or concurrently deleted comment cannot be dismissed (it resolves as `unavailable`).
- Community deletion audit: any comment deletion authorized only by the `community` ACL must create, in the same transaction, the deterministic immutable record `picks/{pickId}/commentDeletionAudit/{commentId}` with exactly `source_comment_id`, `authorUid`, `text`, `created_by` (Auth UID) and server `created_at`. Rules bind it to the existing source, require the source to be gone after the write, reject audits without deletion, client timestamps, altered evidence or extra fields, forbid update/delete, restrict reads to community admins, and block recreating a comment with an audited ID. It works when the pick is missing. The mural and the report queue (`removed`) both use this transaction; community admins always leave an audit, even when they own the pick. Intentional unaudited exceptions: the comment author deleting their own comment and the pick owner moderating their own mural. Test fixtures that delete as community admins must also clean up the exact audit path.
- This reporting phase uses local Security Rules, not deployed rules. The focused independent security review confirmed the deletion-audit and dismissal-consistency corrections without new high-confidence findings; this is not a security certification. Focused tests: `npx --no-install playwright test --config=viewer-preview.playwright.config.js comment-reports comment-report-ui --workers=1 --reporter=list` (Chromium, Firefox and WebKit; specs override baseURL to the existing loopback preview on 5502; only `demo-fijas-vivo` Auth 9099/Firestore 8080). Coverage includes pure input/resolution logic, real SDK privacy/schema/identity checks, idempotence, source disappearance, atomic review/audit/races, inline keyboard/mobile/reduced-motion UI, retry and session/thread/ACL/unmount cleanup. Fixtures use unique temporary identities and exact document cleanup, never global seed/reset. Desktop/mobile screenshots are test artifacts in Playwright's configured temporary output directory; lint and `npm run build:css` remain required.
- Verification note: the earlier `community.spec.js` setup failure came from the protected 5500 preview, not this feature; that regression runs with `viewer-preview.playwright.config.js` (5510). Reporting UI tests also use this static test server to isolate multi-step drafts from the save-triggered reloads of the interactive preview on 5502; reporting SDK tests retain 5502. Resolution tests wait for confirmed transaction feedback before changing filters, not just a row disappearing during reload. The interactive preview remains available separately. No emulator services were restarted or global fixtures reset. The retry UI test injects one rejected SDK transaction-read response, then removes that transport fault and commits against the real emulator; security/atomicity tests do not mock Firestore.
- All roles sign in through the frontpage using the shared Firebase Auth instance. The account dropdown watches effective permissions from `platformAdmins` and approved `perfiles`, never trusting `users.role`. Authorized dashboards mount natively in the central column without navigation or dashboard iframes; the navbar, feed DOM, filters and feed scroll remain intact. “Regresar al streaming”, browser history and live permission revocation unmount subscriptions and restore the viewer context. The tipster OBS preview is the only embedded iframe. The standalone dashboard URLs remain available; unauthenticated tipster entry returns to the unified login.
- Profile settings are a native central view available to every active account, with verified email required to save. `users/{uid}/settings/profile` privately stores the viewer display name, HTTPS avatar URL, short bio, Emerald/contrast theme and accessible community announcement preference. These settings do not modify Auth identity, administrative authority or the tipster's editorial profile; there is no upload, push/email notification service or public viewer-profile directory. The navbar reflects saved settings in real time.
- Tipster performance KPIs cover the selected bankroll date period, not lifetime totals. Administrative registered-user totals count base viewer documents, including privileged accounts with modular permissions; the total is unavailable without the existing viewer-read permission. Mural moderation settings are available only to super admins and admins with explicit `community` access; private comment reports and their review queue are described above.
- Tipster history shows a compact analysis icon only for picks with a published note, opening a keyboard-accessible floating popover. OBS selection uses a checkbox with saving feedback and stays editable for started and settled picks without modifying their terms, results or statistics. The widget panel has a direct action to manage selection, returning keyboard focus to the history. Public presentation lives separately in `perfiles/{uid}/obsSelections/{pickId}` with `visible` and server-owned `updated_at`; writes require the verified, active, approved owner, current legal acceptance and an existing owned pick. The overlay honors explicit selection over legacy `show_on_stream`, including explicit hiding, and listens to selected public picks in groups of at most 30. Future editable-pick deletion removes its presentation record atomically. Reads of absent picks permit deletion notifications; existing picks retain approval-based access. Profile revocation stops the overlay and hides its content. No production migration or deployment occurs; the updated rules are a reviewed prototype requiring validation before wider release.
- New public picks use `event_date`, `sport`, `event`, `league`, `market`, `selection`, `odds`, `stake` (public units), `bookmaker`, `analysis` and `status` (`pending`, `won`, `lost`, `void`, `cashed_out`). `cashout_value` is gross returned public units, or `cashout_odds` is a gross-return/stake multiplier; neither represents private currency. Unit ROI/Yield weights actual settled stake, excludes void/pending and incomplete legacy returns; cashout is excluded from win rate. Private bankroll ROI continues to use the separately recorded monetary return and never infers it from public units.
- `pick-schema.js` adapts historical aliases at read time without modifying documents or inventing stake/return. Tipster history merges indexed canonical and legacy date queries; legacy date indexes must remain until an explicit migration. When an owner writes an eligible legacy document, it is replaced with the canonical public whitelist while retaining creation time and unchanged terms during settlement. Critical terms lock after event start; a pending result may be settled once even after start, then cannot be reopened. Historical missing league/market/stake stay empty/null on a result-only upgrade and are excluded from unit profitability as necessary. No automatic production migration or deployment occurs.

## Brand Commitments

- Product name: Fijas en vivo.
- Frontpage panel controls live in their own sidebars. Directional arrows and hover/focus tooltips indicate collapse or expansion; collapsed desktop panels retain a narrow control rail, and the mobile tipster rail retains its reopen button. The mobile mural uses its existing drawer and launcher. Dashboard auto-hiding remains unchanged.
- Existing identity and interface copy are Spanish-language.

## Evidence on Hand

- The working implementation is in `index.html`, `admin.html`, and `owner.html`.
- Local preview uses the VS Code task “Fijas: vista previa automatica” at loopback port 5502 with automatic SSE reload; preview scripts are local tooling and are not published.
- Firebase configuration, Firestore rules, and indexes are in the project root.
- No user testimonials, performance claims, partnerships, or paid-plan commitments are established.

## Product Principles

- Make public prediction ownership and status clear.
- Let each tipster control which predictions appear in their stream.
- Keep publishing permissions enforced by Firestore, not just by interface visibility.
- Do not imply paid features or exclusive predictions before a trusted payment system exists.
